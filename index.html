01 Building now

Three products, one thread: AI that acts on the real world, not just answers about it.

In design · 2026

Akasha.ai

The operational intelligence layer for Indian enterprises.

Factories, warehouses and construction sites already own cameras, an ERP and a pile of documents. None of it talks, and nobody watches it in real time. Akasha turns that existing investment into one queryable, continuously updated picture of the business, with agents that act on it.

Eye
Edge CV on the customer's own GPU. Camera feeds in, typed events out — PPE_VIOLATION, FIRE_DETECTED, VEHICLE_ENTRY. Raw video never leaves the site.
Brain
Operational memory in one Postgres: pgvector for search, Apache AGE for the entity graph. Ask "PPE violations in Sector 4 this month, by shift" and get SQL-backed answers with citations.
Action
A LangGraph runtime wired to both: agents that trigger on camera events, read memory and send the WhatsApp, the PDF, the Tally entry.
Replay an incident 00.0 s
  1. EyeCamera 14, Sector 4. Frame decoded in the edge container (TensorRT).
  2. EyeSmoke classified. FIRE_DETECTED published to the event bus.
  3. BrainZone resolved to its supervisor and 14-day history (~80 ms query).
  4. ActionFire-response agent wakes on the event.
  5. ActionBilingual incident report written; severity trend attached.
  6. DoneWhatsApp to the safety officer, PDF filed, memory updated. No human started any of it.

Chef.ai

Scan your fridge. Cook something good. Share it.

Point a phone at the fridge: YOLO-Seg finds the ingredients, Gemini reads freshness and cuisine fit, and a LangGraph pipeline matches them against a 7,300-recipe corpus. ChefGPT then stays with you at the stove over WebSocket, with nine tools for cooking steps, meal plans, shopping lists and health goals. Zero custom training — every decision is an agent's.

A voice-first safety layer for getting home in urban India.

Artemis scores every route against real incident data, keeps a live heatmap that decays with age and shifts with time of day, and runs a full SOS protocol: GPS broadcast to trusted contacts every 10 seconds, WhatsApp location share, emergency services. Tribes lets a private group see each other move. I lead a 12-person team taking it from MVP to a business.

03 Research

Doing more with less: smaller models, sparser inputs, explanations you can measure.

Under review ACMM 2026 · with Dr. Gargi Srivastava

LAST: a Lightweight Adaptive-Shift Transformer for real-time activity recognition

Video transformers recognise actions well and run badly on a phone. LAST drops dense pixels for a sparse skeleton, then recovers the lost context three ways:

  • Adaptive graph convolutions learn joint-to-joint edges the body doesn't have — a waving hand and the head.
  • Temporal shift modules mix neighbouring frames at zero parameter cost.
  • Linear attention brings the transformer to O(N), and distillation from VideoMAE V2 keeps the semantics.
